# Prototype and Dogfood Capability

Activate when the dominant uncertainty is what users need, how an interaction should feel, whether a workflow is feasible, or whether a product idea deserves durable implementation.

## Goal

Buy information cheaply before buying architecture.

## Cycle

1. Define the question the prototype must answer.
2. Build the smallest reversible end-to-end path that can answer it.
3. Put it in front of representative real use or a faithful simulation.
4. Observe behavior and collect specific friction, failure, and value signals.
5. Decide: discard, iterate, or productionize.
6. If productionizing, re-enter the normal Outcome Kernel and harden only what the evidence now justifies.

Do not let prototype shortcuts silently become production architecture. Mark disposable assumptions and temporary data paths explicitly when they could otherwise be mistaken for durable design.

## Dogfood

Prefer dogfood when the team itself is a representative user. Instrument the behavior that matters rather than relying only on verbal impressions. For UI/interaction work, exercise the actual interface when possible; source inspection is insufficient evidence for perceptual quality.

## Human judgment

Keep humans at product-intent and taste decisions. Agents may synthesize feedback, cluster issues, and propose experiments, but they do not convert ambiguous preference into product policy without human authority.
